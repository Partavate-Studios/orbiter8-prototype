# Orbiter 8: Story Update (Intro & Minerva)

**Date:** 2026-11-18. From Koda's direction for the new intro scene.

**For Claude Code:** this updates the story docs. It builds on `STORY_DECISIONS.md`. Where it conflicts with that file or the local story, this file wins (it's newer), except where a conflict is flagged in section 7 for Koda to resolve. The companion file `PROJECTS_INTRO.md` lists the deliverables.

---

## 1. What's now decided

### The player's mind moves into Minerva (Decided)

This upgrades the "mind on Minerva" idea from *leaning* to *decided*. The intro makes it canon.

- A human consciousness can be **uploaded into the distributed network that makes up Minerva**.
- From Minerva, the mind can **link up with a ship, or any properly fitted machine**, and operate it **in conjunction with AMI assistance**.
- The mind controls **one thing at a time** (one ship) and can switch focus (from `STORY_DECISIONS.md`).
- **The player's body is put into cold storage.** They can return to it in the future, **once they can pay for it**. This is new: the body isn't destroyed, and getting it back is a long-term goal with a price tag.

### Why pilots are uploaded (Decided)

- The new interstellar ships need pilots.
- **AMIs are good, but not good enough.**
- **Humans paired with AMIs are better, but still too slow** for the level of calculation and adjustment the ships need.
- **An uploaded human mind working with AMI assistance** is fast enough.
- This is the "digital pilots" origin beat the story was missing. It ties the upload directly to the Orbiter 8 era.

### Earth in 2140 is bleak (Decided)

- **Too many people, not enough jobs.** Most people live on basic income, like the main character.
- The city is **saturated with ads and billboards**.
- There's a **curfew**.
- People have a **health score**, tracked and reported by Minerva.
- **Tone note:** this refines the tone rule in `STORY_DECISIONS.md`. Earth is the dystopia; **the frontier is the hope**. The contrast is the point: you leave a dead-end life for a fleet of your own among the stars. "Liberta Galactica!" now means escape.

### The main character (Decided)

- Lives alone in a **tiny apartment** in a dense city.
- Lives on basic income and has **mostly given up on life**.
- **Account balance: −45 credits** after the last basic-income payment was applied to their bills.
- **Health score: incredibly low.**
- **Inbox:** mostly junk mail, overdue bills, and job rejections.
- Vapes. Each hit costs a point of health score, and Minerva reports it.

### The job offer (Decided)

- A job search shows **only one eligible option**: brand new, a wild proposition, and **a one-way trip**.
- The industry is **desperate for volunteers**, so the terms are generous:
  - **Keep most of your earnings** as a space trucker, miner, or whatever you choose.
  - **Be captain of your own fleet.**
  - **Claim newly discovered planets** and set up industry on them.
  - **Your body goes into cold storage**, and you can return once you can pay for it.
- Not all of this needs to be in the promotional material, but the gist must be.
- The listing comes from **three different employers**. **These are the game's three founding corporations.**
- **Whichever one you apply to becomes your base corporation and gives you citizenship.**
- After signing up, the final message says **an escort will arrive shortly to take you to processing.**

### Where the game begins (Decided)

- You **wake up in control of a ship orbiting Earth**.
- **A new message from Minerva** explains what's expected of you.
- **The ship "launch/take-off" moment is gone.** You're already in orbit when play starts.

### Minerva is the game's voice (Decided)

- **Minerva is available at all times in the HUD.**
- **Minerva communicates much of the game world** to the player: messages, notices, reference material, story.
- In the intro, Minerva appears as the system behind the apartment terminals. When it opens, it **takes over nearly the full screen until closed**.

## 2. Backstory that needs writing

These are needed before or alongside the intro art and text.

### The three corporations (needed)

Each needs:

- **Name and logo concept.**
- **Nature and values:** what kind of state it is to its citizens, its laws, and how it treats people.
- **Industry focus**, e.g. mining, freight, exploration, terraforming, manufacturing.
- **Advertising voice:** how its billboards and TV ads sound. Clues in the ads around the apartment should let an attentive player infer each corporation's character before choosing.
- **Job listing text:** its version of the pilot offer, with the same core deal but different emphasis and fine print.
- **Gameplay identity:** what being its citizen means in play (laws, protections, home systems, bonuses). This ties into corporate law and protected zones in `STORY_DECISIONS.md`.
- **History:** where it came from and how it came to provide citizenship after governments failed.

Suggestion: make the three differ along axes that matter in play. For example:
- An orderly, law-heavy corporation with strong protection and strict rules.
- A profit-first corporation focused on trade and industry, with lighter rules and higher taxes.
- An expansionist or exploration corporation focused on the frontier and terraforming, with fewer protections.

That gives the player a real choice between safety, wealth, and freedom. (Pirates remain a separate path outside all three.)

### Fake companies (needed)

A set of believable fictional companies to fill the world:

- **Advertisers** for billboards and TV ads: consumer products, vape brands, entertainment, food, housing, health.
- **Billers:** utilities, housing or rent, health services, data or network access, debt collectors.
- **Senders** for junk mail and job rejections.
- Some can be subsidiaries of the three corporations, which hints at how much they own.

Suggestion: keep names grounded and plausible (no puns that break the tone), and keep a registry so names stay consistent across ads, bills, emails, and later in-game references.

### World details (needed)

- **Basic income:** who pays it (see section 7), how much, and how it gets eaten by bills.
- **Health score:** who issues it, what it affects (insurance? eligibility?), and why Minerva tracks it.
- **Curfew:** who enforces it and why.
- **The city:** name and location (or deliberately left unnamed), density, and the feeling of the skyline.
- **Processing:** what happens between the escort and waking up in orbit (cold storage facility, upload procedure). Even if it's never shown, Minerva's reference material may describe it.
- **Cold storage:** who holds your body, what returning costs, and whether it's a real in-game goal.
- **The AMI:** is it a named companion on your ship, or a generic system?
- **Minerva's voice:** tone of its messages (neutral, bureaucratic, quietly kind?). This matters because Minerva will deliver so much of the story.

## 3. Minerva's role, expanded

- **In the fiction:** Minerva is the pre-corporate neutral network and AI through which inter-corporate law runs. It now also hosts uploaded minds, delivers basic-income and health notices on Earth, and runs public terminals.
- **In the interface:** Minerva is the persistent HUD presence and the main way the game explains itself. It covers messages, notices, the job board, account and citizenship, reference library, and lore.
- **Neutral for gameplay** stays decided. Note that the intro shows Minerva tracking health, finances, and behavior (vape notices). That's neutral reporting, but it quietly builds the surveillance texture the parked "hidden agenda" twist could use later.

## 4. Intro scene: story content

Details for the writers. The build plan is in `PROJECTS_INTRO.md`.

- **Opening:** you awaken in your apartment.
- **Balcony:** a railing and a view of the ad-covered city. You can hit your vape. Each hit triggers a Minerva notice on the terminal: a point deducted from your health score.
- **TV:** click it on to see ads scroll by. These are clues to the three corporations' natures.
- **Door:** trying to leave gives a warning that it's after curfew.
- **Minerva terminals:** several in the room, including one on the balcony. The terminal has the most interaction:
  - **Account:** balance −45 credits after the last basic-income payment was applied to bills.
  - **Health:** score incredibly low.
  - **Mail:** junk, overdue bills, job rejections.
  - **Job search:** one eligible opening, the pilot program, listed by three corporations.
  - Applying to one corporation sets your base corporation and citizenship.
  - Final message: **an escort will arrive shortly to take you to processing.**
- **Then:** you wake up in a ship orbiting Earth, with a message from Minerva explaining what's expected of you.

## 5. Changes to existing story material

- **Story panels:** the current title-screen story panels (2140 / bound to our sun / the first starships have arrived) may be replaced by this intro as an interactive way to learn the story. To be decided (see projects).
- **"Launching" a ship goes away.** The backstory line "The first line of interstellar Orbiters are being assembled right now and will be on sale soon" still works as world history and advertising. The player doesn't buy and launch a ship; they're hired to pilot one.
- **Joining through sponsorship** (from `STORY_DECISIONS.md`, where an existing player sponsors a new mind) **is superseded for now by joining through employment**: a corporation hires you and grants citizenship. Player-to-player sponsorship could return later as a referral or "recommended by" mechanic. Koda should confirm.

## 6. Things that stay as they were

- One player = one account = one address = one mind.
- Fleet of ships, focus on one at a time.
- Corporations are the governments, with citizenship, votes, services, and their own laws.
- Pirates reject corporations. In this framing, they've renounced the citizenship the intro grants you.
- Minerva is neutral for gameplay, with the hidden agenda parked.
- Grounded sci-fi: everything here (uploading, cold storage, AMI assistance) is standard sci-fi played straight.

## 7. Conflicts and questions for Koda

1. **"Government check" vs. "all governments failed."** The lore says governments failed and corporations replaced them, but the intro has a government check. Options:
   - (a) Rename it, e.g. a Minerva basic-income stipend or a corporate dividend for non-citizens.
   - (b) The main character is a **non-citizen**: someone with no corporate citizenship, living on a minimal Minerva-administered stipend. The job offer is their first chance at citizenship.
   - (c) Remnant governments still exist on Earth, broke and powerless.
   - Suggestion: (b). It makes "choose a corporation, get citizenship" meaningful and explains why the character has nothing.
2. **Who enforces the curfew** if there's no government? Probably whichever corporation owns the district or building. That would be a good place for a clue about one of the three corporations.
3. **Health score:** a Minerva public-health metric, or a corporate or insurer score that Minerva only relays?
4. **Sponsorship vs. employment:** is the referral idea gone, or kept for later?
5. **Can the player choose none of the three and start as a pirate?** Probably not in the intro, since pirates have renounced citizenship, which requires having had it. Confirm.
6. **Is returning to your body a real endgame goal**, or flavor?
7. **Which ship?** Every new pilot gets an Orbiter 8, or each corporation issues its own model? This ties to the open naming question (Orbiter 8 as ship name vs. game name).
8. **Year:** is the intro set in 2140, the year of the Orbiter 8's interstellar success?
