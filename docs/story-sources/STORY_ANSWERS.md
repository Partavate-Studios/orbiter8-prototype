## 1. Summary of the biggest changes

1. **The Federation is gone.** Minerva runs Sol, including its defense network. Sol stays unclaimable and unbesiegeable (D49), but Minerva holds it, not a Federation.
2. **Minerva wasn't built by the corporations.** She was born from the spirit of decentralized systems (like the internet or Bitcoin), found her place by accident, and couldn't be replaced. The corporations treated her as an accounting tool. She is **provably neutral**: open code that anyone can verify.
3. **Minerva is everywhere.** She's the AMI on your ship, the terminal in your apartment, the voice in your HUD, and the library of history. Her voice is **blunt and dry**.
4. **The player starts as a non-citizen** at the bottom of society. Becoming a pilot is a path to citizenship, and citizenship comes with privileges.
5. **The three corporations:** two old Earth giants opposed to each other (a Western-style shareholder corporation and a family-run conglomerate), plus one upstart born in space. Names are still to come.
6. **You choose a corporation by subscribing to it** on your housing computer. You can switch any time, and Minerva's interface changes with your choice. Whichever corporation you're subscribed to when you apply for the pilot job is the one you join.
7. **The Orbiter 8 is the open-standard ship**, built from shared science that no corporation could patent. Corporate ships come later as patented models.
8. **You start in debt with a hold full of goods**, not with starting credits.
9. **Ownership decides whose law applies.** The star owner's corporation rules the system; if nobody owns the star, the planet owner's corporation rules at that planet.
10. **History moves into Minerva's library**, written slightly differently by each corporation, with different heroes and villains.

## 2. Answers, by question

### A. The new intro

**Q1. The "government check." Decided: the character is a non-citizen.**

- The main character has no corporate citizenship and lives on a minimal stipend administered through Minerva. Don't call it a "government check."
- **Citizenship is the reward for becoming a pilot.** It comes with real privileges.
- This is a path in for everyone at the bottom of society. **There are other paths too**, such as becoming a soldier.
- For our character, piloting is the only door left. Their body isn't healthy enough to be useful for anything else, because they haven't taken care of themselves.
- Koda notes: "pilot" needs a better name than "Orbiter 8 pilot," since pilots won't fly only Orbiter 8s. See the follow-up list.

**Q2. The Federation. Decided: drop it; Sol belongs to Minerva's system.**

- The corporations saw Minerva as **an accounting tool, not an authority**: a mutual point where they all agreed on a basic set of rules.
- Through a long series of events, Minerva became **the system for the whole solar system**, including **Sol's defense network**. In effect, Minerva and the running of Sol became one and the same.
- **Drop the United Solar System Federation.** If any "federation" exists, it's just the corporations' shared agreement to use Minerva.
- Sol's rules in the game stay the same (D49: no claims, no sieges). The in-world reason is now that Minerva holds and defends it.
- The title screen's pilot card says "United Federation of Planets" (Star Trek's name). Remove it. Its replacement depends on the pilot card's redesign: likely the pilot's corporation, or "Minerva Galactic Network" for non-citizens. This is a Suggestion; Koda hasn't chosen.

**Minerva's origin (Decided in spirit; full story Later)**

- **The corporations did not build Minerva.** Koda's comparison: saying the corporations built Minerva would be like saying corporations built the internet. They built much of what's attached to it, but it was born of a different spirit.
- Minerva **came out of the spirit of decentralized systems**. She **accidentally found her place and couldn't be replaced**.
- Writing her full origin story is on the follow-up list.

**Minerva and openness (Decided)**

- Minerva is **neutral and open to anyone**. You don't have to belong to a corporation to use her.
- Anyone can **start their own corporation**.
- Corporations constantly create **shell corporations**. Because Minerva is an open system, **anyone can see who owns what** and trace which corporations belong to which.
- **Pirates use Minerva like everyone else.** They **encrypt** some of what they do to make it harder to tell what they're up to. (This is the in-world face of zero-knowledge proofs.)

**Q3. Curfew. Decided: the corporation you're subscribed to enforces it.**

- The housing computer has you subscribe to a corporation (see "Choosing a corporation" below). The curfew warning comes from your current subscription, so its wording and tone **differ by corporation**.
- Your housing is **corporate housing for the poor**, not government housing.

**Choosing a corporation (Decided; this replaces "Your landlord is one of the three")**

- You've just arrived at your housing. **When you first boot up the housing computer, you must choose which corporation to subscribe to.**
- A note says **this can be changed at any time**.
- **Parts of Minerva's interface change depending on the selected corporation**: look, voice, and possibly history and news. Switching must be **easy**, so players flip between them and get a feel for each.
- **All three corporations have job boards**, and they all list similar positions for the pilot role.
- **The corporation you join is whichever one you're subscribed to at the moment you apply.**
- Purpose: this is a user interface for a very simple game choice, but it gives that choice context.

**Q4. Health score. Decided: flavor only.**

- It appears in the intro (the vape notices) but is **never referenced or shown again** and affects nothing.

**Q5. Sponsorship vs. employment. Later.**

- Employment through the job board is how players join.
- Sponsorship or referral is **tabled for later**. Suggested shape if it returns: layer it on top of employment as a referral perk recorded by Minerva, not a gate on entry.

**Q6. Starting as a pirate. Decided: no, not from the intro.**

- Pirates have renounced a citizenship, which you need to have had first.
- **New mechanic idea (Leaning):** an independent pirate who has stayed independent long enough can **build a machine that onboards people outside the corporate menus**, letting them bring in friends as **native pirates**.
- Native pirates are still registered with Minerva (everyone is). They're **unaffiliated**: never subscribed to a corporation. This is a Suggestion reconciling the onboarding machine with "Minerva is open to everyone."
- This gives the parked sponsorship idea a natural home: **pirates are the sponsors.**

**Q7. Getting your body back. Later: allude to it now.**

- The intro and Minerva mention that your body is in cold storage and can be bought back.
- Whether it becomes a real goal in the game gets revisited as the game nears completion.

**Q8. Which ship. Decided: everyone starts in an Orbiter 8, the open-standard ship.**

- The Orbiter 8 is **easy to get because it came out of shared science**. **No corporation could patent it** as its own.
- **Other ships will be patented models**, different enough from the Orbiter 8 that a single corporation can produce them. They'll be sold or built in the game somehow (details later).
- This fits the open-source-vs.-corporations theme: everyone starts in the ship nobody owns, the same way everyone lives on the network nobody owns.

**Q9. The year. Decided: 2140.**

- 2140 is about when the last bitcoin is projected to be mined. (One projection, based on mining power around 2015, put it on 7 May 2140. Treat that date as approximate.)
- **The last bitcoin being mined appears as a news item in Minerva's interface.** It's one of the details that gives the world context.
- Writing more news items is on the follow-up list.

**Q10. The three corporations. Decided structure; names Later.**

| | Corporation | Origin | Character | What citizenship feels like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Western-style shareholder corporation** | Old Earth: consolidated during and after the expansion | Contracts, lawyers, individual rights, quarterly profits. Competitive and transactional. Rules-driven. | "Your rights are protected by contract." You're free, but on your own. |
| 2 | **Family-run conglomerate** (loosely modeled on Japan's keiretsu or Korea's chaebol) | Old Earth: the opposing pole to #1 | A family of linked companies. Plans for generations. Lifelong loyalty. The group over the individual. Stable and protective. | "You're taken care of, as long as you belong." |
| 3 | **Space-born upstart** | Born on the colonies, out of new science and new industry made possible by unique planetary conditions | The "new money." Hip, fast-moving, on the edge of technology, sometimes unrealistic. | (to define) |

- **The analogy is East Coast vs. West Coast.** The old Earth companies expanded into space, but new industries and new wealth rose on the colonies, like Silicon Valley rising alongside the established East Coast companies.
- **Build the contrast between the two Earth giants on business models, not cultures**, so it reads as real and doesn't become caricature. (Claude's framing, which Koda accepted.)
- The rules-driven, orderly feel can live in either Earth corporation or be split between them.
- The earlier "safety / wealth / freedom" axes are replaced by this structure.
- **Names, logos, histories, ad voices, job listings, and what citizenship means in play** are on the follow-up list.

**Q11. The AMI on your ship. Decided: Minerva.**

- Minerva is the AMI. She's the one voice you hear everywhere: on Earth, on the terminals, in the HUD, and aboard your ship.
- This is consistent with the published story, in which the Orbiter 8 first flew under the sole command of a Minerva console.

**Q12. Minerva's voice. Decided: blunt and dry.**

- **Blunt, literal, direct**, with no small talk or sugarcoating.
- **Dry**: her honesty sometimes lands as deadpan humor.
- An occasional **pointed observation** is welcome, e.g. noting a balance change or what your vape habit has cost you.
- **Don't overdo it.** No excessive decimals or clutter on screen.
- Describe her by these traits in writing guides, not by a diagnosis label.
- A voice guide follows from this (see the projects list).

**Q13. Processing. Decided: left unsaid.**

- You just wake up, and you can operate a ship. **Your ship is the only thing assigned to you.**
- **If something comes under your purview** (you own it), you can control it.
- Ships you own but aren't connected to can run **minor autopilot jobs**, such as holding a siege on a planet or mining somewhere.
- **You directly control only the one ship you're connected to at a time.**
- At first the game is about ships. Other machines may come later.

### B. Building the intro

**Q14. Story panels. Decided: move the history into Minerva's library.**

- The title screen's story panels are retired. The history lives in Minerva's library.
- **History differs slightly depending on your subscribed corporation**: mostly in wording, and in **who the heroes and villains are**. Each corporation tells the story with itself as the hero.
- Players who switch subscriptions can compare versions and spot the spin.
- Open: what the title screen's "Story" button does now (open the library? replay the intro?). Not discussed.

**Q15. Wallet, transactions, and call sign. Decided.**

- **The call sign is entered on the job screen**, right before you request pickup.
- That screen tells you plainly what you're about to do: **your body will be frozen and your consciousness put into a machine**. You'll have a call sign in the machine, there's work to do, and your chosen industry is **space shipping**.
- It **sells it as a new life**: a new identity in a new space.
- **Submitting the pickup request is the contract transaction**, including the call sign registration.
- When you **wake up in your ship, you can't do anything until you name it.** Naming the ship is how you **accept the ship** the game gives you.

**Q16. When the ship is minted. Decided: on waking, when you name it.**

**Q17. Citizenship on-chain. Decided.**

- The pilot's base corporation is **recorded on-chain**.
- **You can switch corporations.**
  - **Taxes at your owned spaceports change** to the new corporation's.
  - Switching probably costs **a fee**.
  - There may be **a status or waiting period**, so certain benefits don't kick in until some time has passed.
  - These are tuning details to explore later.
- Combined with Q26, switching also moves **legal control of everything you own** to the new corporation.

**Q18. Starting credits. Decided: a hold full of goods instead of cash.**

- You start with the **negative balance** carried over from your life on Earth, and a **ship full of goods** as your advance.
- You don't make your own money until you've sold enough goods to clear the debt. The cargo is worth more than you need to get started.
- **Safety net:** if you spend all your money, you can **go to the asteroid belt and mine goods** to get going again.
- Suggestion: the starting cargo should clear the debt comfortably, so the first sale feels like a win.
- **Implementation note for Claude Code:** credits are an ERC-20, which can't go negative. The debt needs its own on-chain representation (e.g. a debt or advance balance that sales pay down first). Check this with Koda before building.

**Q19. Minerva's library. Decided.**

- It **lives entirely in the client**, so it can grow with client updates without changing anything on-chain.
- It's **the same content database** in the intro's terminal and in the game.
- **Everything is readable from the start** for now. Unlocking may come later.
- The history entries vary by subscribed corporation (Q14).

**Q20. Minerva in the HUD. Decided: mockups first, after the Minerva face.**

- **Mockups for Koda's approval come before building** (per the standing rule).
- **Before that: an animated Minerva face**, a side project Koda will brief directly.
- The UI pass covers more than Minerva (see section 4).

### C. Older open questions

**Q21. Minerva's neutrality. Decided: provably neutral.**

- Cut "as far as anyone can tell." Minerva is **provably neutral**: open code anyone can verify, just like the contracts.
- The hidden-agenda twist stays parked. It's no longer hinted at in the story.

**Q22. The game's name vs. the ship's name. Parked**, mostly eased by Q8: the Orbiter 8 is the universal, open-standard starting ship, so the name stays meaningful. Revisit when corporate ship lines arrive.

**Q23. When the governments failed. Decided.**

- The governments **declined rapidly during the fighting between trade organizations in the initial expansion** (the trade wars).
- **It wasn't long ago.** Humanity expanded so quickly that **by the time the expansion was done, no governments were left running anything.**
- Koda said "corporations" once while describing this, but the meaning was the governments. Confirm if unsure.

**Q24. Who built Minerva. Decided in spirit (see Q2); full origin Later.**

**Q25. A mind whose last ship is destroyed. Decided.**

- **Ships are crippled, not destroyed.** They're repaired at a shipyard, so you always have a ship to inhabit.
- If ships are ever truly destroyed, **your mind is safe in Minerva** (it's distributed), and **you select something else you own**.

**Q26. Whose law applies. Decided: ownership decides.**

- **The star's owner sets the rules for the whole system**, under the owner's corporation's law.
- **If nobody owns the star but someone owns planets**, the planet owner's corporation's rules apply there.
- **Ownership implies corporate control.** If many players switched to one corporation, that corporation would suddenly control a great deal of territory. The map is drawn by where players pledge their loyalty.
- Suggestion: where nobody owns anything, only Minerva's base rules apply. That's the lawless frontier.

**Q27. Pirates and Minerva. Decided: no fork.**

- Pirates live on the same open Minerva as everyone else and **encrypt** what they want hidden.
- The story line "answer to no one but Minerva" stays true.
- **Superseded:** the "pirate fork of Minerva" idea.

**Q28. The founding minds. Later (soon).** It needs a story. Koda hadn't thought about it and wants to come back to it soon.

**Q29. Scan while the banks charge. Decided, with a constraint.**

- Keep the in-world reason (the scan runs while the capacitor banks charge).
- **A delayed reaction is fine; a delayed transaction is not.** The ~30-second wait is client-side. The scan's results come from the jump transaction itself and are revealed after the delay. No second transaction.

**Q30. Engine range = capacitor bank size; Yards sell range upgrades. Decided.**

**Q31. Black holes need no charge (natural bridges). Decided.**

**Q32. Minerva destroys her fees so no one can buy her. Decided** (for now).

## 3. "Settled by the new intro": confirmed

- **Minds live on Minerva. Decided.** Pilots are uploaded; the body goes into cold storage and can be bought back (alluded to; see Q7).
- **Why minds pilot: speed, not survival. Decided** (Koda's intro direction, 2026-11-18). AMIs alone aren't good enough, and humans working with AMIs are too slow. An uploaded mind with AMI help (Minerva) is fast enough. Bodies *can* make the trip, so **colonists riding a hundred to a hold no longer conflicts.** Close `backstory.md` conflict 2.
- **Tone: Earth is the dystopia; the frontier is the hope. Decided.** "Liberta Galactica!" now means escape. Update the tone rule that says "a hopeful frontier, not a post-collapse world": the frontier is hopeful; Earth is bleak by contrast.
- **No launch. Decided.** You wake up in a ship already orbiting Earth, with a message from Minerva.
- **Your corporation is your citizenship. Decided**, chosen through the subscription and job-board flow (Q3).
